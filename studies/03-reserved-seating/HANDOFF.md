## 1. The request, and how it was interpreted

**Owner's request (2026-09-13), in substance:**

- A new study, very similar to study 02 (ticket selling). Study 02 was for shows without
  marked seats, such as a football stadium. Study 03 sells **marked seats**.
- A buyer **selects seats and keeps them for some minutes (e.g. 40)** to finish the
  purchase, and must not find out, while paying, that a seat is no longer available.
- Review study 02 so that its description, texts, nomenclature and tests are right and
  coherent. The owner expects the changes to be mostly naming ("ticket numbers are
  essentially seat numbers") and delegated the decisions.

**Decided interpretation:**

1. **Study 02 is general admission, and stays as measured.** Its README gets a
   terminology section and one clarified sentence (Part A, §2). No SQL, harness, report or
   analysis changes, and no re-run. The review found no test that is wrong under general
   admission.
2. **Study 03 is reserved seating.** Buyers choose specific seats and hold them for 40
   minutes. It is not study 02 renamed. Marking seats moves the hard part of the problem,
   and the README must say why:
   - In general admission the invariant (*sold ≤ capacity*) spans many rows, so the design
     question is where the capacity check lives. With marked seats, *a seat is sold at most
     once* is a fact about one row, and a primary key or unique index already enforces it.
     Overbooking an event stops being the question.
   - The new hard parts:
     1. **A promise that lasts 40 minutes.** A hold must exclude everyone else until it
        expires, and then give the seat back.
     2. **Two buyers want the same seat, not just any seat.** `SKIP LOCKED`, study 02's
        fastest correct tool, cannot apply: nobody may skip the seat they chose. Conflicts
        become visible to users ("that seat was just taken, choose again").
     3. **Multi-seat selections must be all-or-nothing.**
     4. **The seat map is the hot read.**
     5. **Expiry and payment race each other.**
     6. **Somebody's clock decides when a hold has expired.**

### 3.2 Glossary (put it in the study 03 README)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| reserved seating | every ticket is for one physical seat, chosen by the buyer |
| general admission | study 02: tickets are interchangeable units of capacity |
| venue seat | a physical place: venue, section, row, seat number (`venue_seat`) |
| event seat | a venue seat for one event: the unit of inventory |
| block | 1–6 adjacent seats in one row of one section, requested together |
| hold | an exclusive, time-limited claim on a block. The owner's "40-minute reservation" is a hold with a 40-minute TTL. (Study 02's table was called `reservation`; study 03 says "hold" throughout.) |
| `expires_at` | end of a hold, judged by the **database clock** unless a design says otherwise (E0) |
| pre-payment check | the buyer's "is my hold still valid?" before paying: friendly, not a guarantee |
| checkout | from pressing *pay* to confirmation. **Payment window** (K1 only): a bounded extension granted when checkout starts |
| confirm | turn a valid hold into tickets, atomically for every seat of the block |
| release | a hold ends without a sale: explicitly (buyer removes the seats) or by expiry |
| steal | a new hold taking a seat whose previous hold has **expired** (legitimate) |
| **theft** | a new hold or sale taking a seat whose previous hold had **not** expired (violation) |
| sweeper | background job: garbage-collects expired holds (most designs) or makes expiry take effect (E1) |
| seat map | a section's seats with state available / held / sold, as a buyer sees them |
| honored hold | the owner's guarantee: a confirmation made while the hold is valid succeeds |

### 3.3 Product policy — identical for every design (Decided)

1. A hold covers one block (1–6 adjacent seats, one row, one section). All-or-nothing.
2. TTL = **40 minutes** from the grant, by the database clock.
3. A hold has expired when `db_now >= expires_at`. **Strict expiry:** a confirmation after
   expiry is refused even if nobody took the seat. A grace policy is a product decision this
   study does not measure; the README says so.
4. The buyer may release a hold explicitly.
5. K1 only: starting checkout while the hold is valid extends it to
   `max(expires_at, now + 10 min)`, once per hold.
6. One price per event. Price zones are out of scope.
7. A sold seat can be refunded (cancel), which makes it available again.

### 3.4 Invariants — "the tests" (Decided)

Every timestamp used by an invariant comes from the **database clock** and is returned by
the statement that did the work (`RETURNING now() AS db_now`, or the harness's own
`SELECT now()` inside the same transaction; §5.4). On PostgreSQL `now()` is the transaction
start time; the engine probe (§4) checks the same holds on YugabyteDB.

| Id | Invariant | Detected by | Violation when | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| INV-1 | **No double sale.** At most one ticket per event seat, and no two buyers told they bought the same seat | DB audit (`a_tickets`: seats with more than one ticket) plus the harness ledger (per seat: at most one live "you bought it"; the ticket's customer is the buyer who was told) | any duplicate, or a mismatch beyond ambiguous commits | after every writing phase |
| INV-2 | **Hold exclusivity (no theft).** Validity intervals of holds on a seat never overlap | ledger: for hold *h* on a seat, the interval is `[granted_at, end)`, with `end = min(expires_at, released_at, confirmed_at)`. A confirmed hold's seat stays taken until a refund. The next grant (or sale) on that seat must have `granted_at >= end` | any overlap | after every writing phase |
| INV-3 | **Honored hold.** A confirmation made while the hold is valid succeeds | ledger: margin `m = expires_at − txn_now` of every **rejected** confirmation (K1: rejected checkout start) | `m >= G` (guard margin, default 2 human minutes = 5% of TTL) is an **early rejection** = violation. `0 < m < G` is a **boundary rejection** (reported cost, not a violation). `m <= 0` is a **late rejection** (expected) | lifecycle, race (deferred confirmers) |
| INV-4 | **No leaked seats.** A seat whose hold ended can be held again | leak probe (§3.9.6): at quiescence, after the final sweep, a probe customer tries to hold each unsold seat without a valid hold | the probe is refused | end of every lifecycle event, end of race |
| INV-5 | **All-or-nothing holds and confirmations** | DB audit `a_valid_holds` vs ledger: every live granted hold is present with exactly its seats; no DB hold unknown to the ledger. Tickets per confirmed hold = seats in the hold | any partial hold or partial sale | after every writing phase |
| INV-6 | **No late sale.** A confirmation at `txn_now >= expires_at` never produces tickets (K1: the extended expiry) | ledger: successful confirmations with `m <= 0` | any | lifecycle, race |
| INV-7 | **Ledger reconciliation** | DB tickets and valid holds vs what the harness saw commit, allowing the recorded ambiguous commits (study 02's rule) | any unexplained difference | after every writing phase |
| INV-8 | **No under-selling in the race** | after the crowd, the deferred confirmations and a final single-seat sweep buyer: unsold seats in events that did not time out | any | race |

**Violations gate timing** exactly as in study 02. A cell whose correct design violates
INV-1..INV-8 still writes its result, but the report marks every number of that phase ❌.
A failed correctness gate (§3.9.1) aborts the cell.

### 3.8 Dataset (Decided)

Deterministic, seed 42, the same in every cell. Tiers are **10, 100, 1 000, 10 000 and
100 000 seats**, as in study 02.

**Venues.** One venue per tier; every event of a tier is at that venue.

| Tier | Sections | Rows per section | Seats per row | Seats per section | Venue name |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 10 | 1 | 1 | 10 | 10 | back room |
| 100 | 1 | 5 | 20 | 100 | club |
| 1 000 | 4 | 10 | 25 | 250 | theatre |
| 10 000 | 20 | 20 | 25 | 500 | arena |
| 100 000 | 100 | 40 | 25 | 1 000 | stadium |

- `seat_id` runs 1..capacity per venue, in (section, row, seat) order.
- `quality_rank` is 1..capacity with no randomness. Sort by section number (section 1 is
  the most desirable), then row number (row 1 nearest the stage), then distance from the
  row's centre, then seat number.

**Event kinds and counts:**

| Kind | Purpose | `small` | `tiny` |
|---|---|---|---|
| catalogue | partly sold, with live and expired holds; reads, isolated writes | 400×10, 80×100, 16×1k, 3×10k, 1×100k | 40×10, 8×100, 2×1k, 1×10k |
| race | unsold; hot drop | 100×10, 20×100, 5×1k, 2×10k, 1×100k | 20×10, 4×100, 1×1k, 1×10k |
| lifecycle | unsold; the hold guarantee under compressed time | 10×10, 10×100, 10×1k | 4×10, 2×100, 1×1k |

`small` holds 40 bands and 20 000 customers; `tiny` holds 10 bands and 2 000. Bands and
customers are Zipf-skewed, as in study 02.

**Catalogue initial state** (generated in Go, deterministic):

1. **Sold:** a fraction `f ~ U[0.2, 0.6]` per event. Choose seats by
   `quality_rank + U[0, 0.3 × capacity]` ascending (best seats sell first, with noise). One
   ticket per seat; loaded tickets have `hold_id` NULL.
2. **Live holds:** 4% of the remaining seats, as blocks of 1–4 adjacent free seats in a row.
   Zipf customer; `expires_at = load_now + U[10, 40] minutes` (real minutes).
3. **Expired holds:** 1% of the remaining seats, same shape, with
   `expires_at = load_now − U[1, 60] minutes`, never garbage-collected.
4. `load_now` is read from the database (`SELECT now()`) right before the bulk copy. Every
   loaded timestamp and the Go ground truth derive from it.
5. Ids: loaded holds get `1..H` and loaded tickets `1..T`. The harness allocates hold and
   ticket ids from 1 000 000 000 upwards, as study 02 did.

Each design lays out this logical state in its own tables (§6). For E1, the loaded
expired holds are `status='held'` rows; the harness runs the sweeper to completion
**before** verification (the design's steady state). This is documented in the gate output.

### 3.9 Experiments and phases (Decided)

Phases, in order: `verify, explain, read, write, race, lifecycle`. Every writing phase
starts from a fresh load and is audited on the state it produced (study 01 and 02 lessons).

#### 3.9.1 `verify` — correctness gate

Before the checks, assert `min(expires_at of loaded live holds) − db_now >= 5 minutes`.
Otherwise the gate fails with "load took too long; loaded holds too close to expiry".

Checks against Go-computed truth. The expiry state is exact thanks to that margin:

- Per tier, on the catalogue event with the most seats sold: `q01` for the section with the
  most sold seats and for the section with the most live holds. `q02` and `q03` for that event.
- Per tier, on one unsold race event: `q02`, `q03`.
- `q04` for the hold with the most seats among live loaded holds, and for one **expired**
  hold (must return no rows).
- `q05` for the customer with the most tickets; `q06` for 20 random loaded tickets.
- Audit on load (INV-1, INV-5, INV-7 against the loaded ledger).

Any failure aborts the cell with no timings (methodology 5).

#### 3.9.2 `explain`

Plans for every read and every write statement. Keys: the largest catalogue event, its
section with the most sold seats, a live hold, a sold ticket. Writes run in rolled-back
transactions, exactly as study 02's `ExplainAll`, using `inspect.Explain` and
`ExplainSequence`.

#### 3.9.3 `read`

Each query in isolation (8 workers, `-duration`/`-warmup`, fresh key per execution):

- `q01`, `q02` and `q03` once per tier, with keys drawn from that tier's catalogue events
  (and, for `q01`, a random section of them);
- `q04`, from live loaded holds;
- `q05`, from customers with tickets;
- `q06`, from loaded tickets.

#### 3.9.4 `write` — isolated, each on a fresh load (`-write-ops hold,release,cancel,publish`)

| Op | What | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| `hold` | spread demand: buyers (§3.10) on catalogue events weighted by available seats; hold, then confirm at once | finite pool: `measure.FiniteBudget(available seats / 2.5, trials)` |
| `release` | explicit release of loaded live holds | finite pool of loaded live holds |
| `cancel` | refund of loaded tickets | finite pool of loaded tickets |
| `publish` | publish an event of each tier (smallest first) | count-based, as study 02 |

#### 3.9.5 `race` — hot drop with seat choice (headline)

For each tier, for each race event, in the style of study 02's `raceEvent`:

1. **Arrival.** 32 buyer goroutines (`-race-buyers`) wait behind a gate. Each is a queue of
   customers using the buyer model (§3.10). Holds use the **real** TTL of 40 minutes
   (`-hold-ttl 40m`).
2. **Confirmation.** A granted hold is confirmed immediately. The exception is **10% of
   granted holds (`-race-defer-pct 10`), the deferred confirmers**: they keep the hold
   until the crowd has finished. The owner's scenario is tested literally: the seats were
   chosen, the sell-out storm happens around them, and they pay at the end.
3. **After the crowd.** Every deferred hold is confirmed. **Every one must succeed** (INV-3;
   none can be near expiry).
4. **Final sweep.** One buyer with party size 1 buys any seat left until `q03` reports 0.
5. **Close.** Run the leak probe for any unsold seat (INV-4), then the under-selling check
   (INV-8), then the audit.
6. **Deadlines.** `-race-timeout 60s` stops new holds, and retries stop at the deadline, as
   in study 02. The deferred wave and in-flight work always finish, bounded by the attempt
   deadline plus 10 s. `-race-tier-budget 3m`.

There is **no organiser editor**: no study 03 design writes the `event` row on the booking
path, so it would measure nothing. The README says so.

Metrics per tier:

- events and seats: events, events planned, seats, sold;
- holds and conflicts: holds granted, hold attempts, conflicts, conflicts per granted hold,
  map reads (`q01`) per granted hold, engine retries, buyers who gave up (20 conflicts),
  buyers who left with no block of their size;
- speed: `allocate_s` (from the gate to the last grant) and `seats_per_sec` (seats granted
  over `allocate_s`);
- latencies: hold (success), conflict, immediate confirmation, deferred confirmation;
- correctness: deferred confirmations attempted, succeeded and early-rejected; timed-out
  events and the share of their seats sold; INV violations; under-sold seats; audit.

#### 3.9.6 `lifecycle` — the hold guarantee under compressed time

**Time scale.** Durations are defined in **human time** and multiplied by
`-human-minute` (the wall-clock length of one human minute). Defaults: 50 ms on PostgreSQL
and 500 ms on YugabyteDB (study.env), calibrated by §10.1.

| Human-time parameter | Flag | Default |
|---|---|---|
| hold TTL | `-lc-ttl` | 40m |
| basket time (choose → pay or leave) | fixed mixture | 70% U[1m,10m] · 20% U[10m,30m] · 10% U[30m,45m] |
| abandoned holds | `-lc-abandon-pct` | 25%, of which `-lc-abandon-explicit-pct` 40% are released explicitly at their basket time; the rest are left to expire |
| payment duration | fixed | U[30s, 4m] |
| payment window (K1) | `-lc-payment-window` | 10m |
| guard margin *G* (INV-3) | `-lc-guard` | 2m |
| sweeper interval | `-lc-sweep-every` | 1m (batch 200) |
| sweeper outage | `-lc-outage-from`, `-lc-outage-to` | 45m → 85m after the event starts; first event of each tier only; `0` disables |
| E0 injected clock skew | `-lc-e0-skew` | 10m (25% of TTL) |
| hungry buyer retry | fixed | 1m after "no block available" |
| event timeout | `-lc-timeout` | 240m |

Tiers and events: PostgreSQL `-lifecycle-tiers 10,100,1000 -lifecycle-events-per-tier 6`;
YugabyteDB `10,100` and `3` (study.env). 32 buyers per event (`-lifecycle-buyers`).

**Buyer flow per customer:**

1. Choose and **hold** a block (§3.10), TTL 40m. With no block available, wait 1m and try
   again until the event ends.
2. Sleep for the basket time.
3. Abandon? Explicitly (release, then leave) or silently (leave).
4. Otherwise run the **pre-payment check** (`w_check_hold`). Invalid → "expired at check";
   the buyer is told before paying and leaves.
5. **K1 only:** run `w_begin_checkout`. Rejected → "expired at checkout"; the buyer leaves.
6. Sleep for the payment duration.
7. **Confirm.** Success, or rejection classified late / boundary / early (INV-3). A
   successful confirmation with `m <= 0` is a late sale (INV-6).

**Sweeper:** runs every `-lc-sweep-every` for **every** design. For E1 it makes expiry take
effect; for the others it garbage-collects.

**Sweeper outage and outage probe.** The sweeper stops at 45m. At 85m the harness pauses
every buyer before their next database operation (a barrier; sleeping buyers keep their
timers). It probes each seat whose latest ledger hold expired ≥ 2 human minutes earlier,
unreleased, unconfirmed and not superseded: one single-seat hold by a probe customer, with
a 1m TTL.

- A probe that is **granted** is recorded in the ledger and released at once.
- A probe that is **refused** is counted as `outage_unavailable_seats`.

Then the sweeper and the buyers resume. The expected result is E1 > 0 (the control
condition fires) and 0 for every lazy design. A lazy design with > 0 is an INV-4 violation.

**End of event.** The event ends when all seats are sold (monitor through `a_event_sold`,
every max(50 ms, human-minute)) or at the timeout. Then:

1. Buyers stop; in-flight work finishes.
2. Wait until every unconfirmed hold has expired (at most TTL + payment window).
3. Run the sweeper until it releases nothing.
4. **Leak probe:** every seat the ledger shows unsold with no live hold, capped at 200
   random seats per event plus every seat whose last hold expired. Refused → leaked (INV-4
   violation).
5. Audit.

Metrics per tier:

- holds and abandonment: holds granted, conflicts, abandoned silently and explicitly;
- checks: expired at check, expired at checkout (K1), payments started;
- sales: confirmed holds and seats, confirmed seats/s over wall time;
- rejections: late, boundary, early;
- violations: thefts, late sales, double sales, leaked seats;
- sweeper: seats released and release lag (from `expires_at` to release, in human minutes,
  p50/p99); outage unavailable seats;
- idle held seat-minutes (from abandoned holds, in human minutes);
- latencies: hold, check, begin-checkout, confirm, release, sweep batch;
- audit.

`-lifecycle-trials N` repeats on fresh loads, as `-race-trials` does.

### 3.10 Buyer model (shared by race, lifecycle and the `hold` write op)

**Party size** `n`: 1 → 20%, 2 → 45%, 3 → 10%, 4 → 20%, 6 → 5%.

**Static layout.** The venue layout (sections, rows, seat numbers, quality ranks) is
**static and cached by the client**, loaded once per venue from `venue_seat`, like a
seat-map image served from a CDN. Only dynamic state is read from the database.

**Loop per customer:**

```
read q02 (per-section availability)
candidates = sections with available >= n, weighted Zipf(s=1.1) over section number
repeat:
  pick a candidate section
  read q01 (non-available seats of that section); merge with the cached layout
  blocks = every run of n adjacent free seats in one row; rank by sum of quality_rank
  if no block: drop the section; after 3 dropped sections re-read q02;
               if no candidates are left: the customer leaves "no block of n"
  pick block k ~ Zipf(s=1.2) over the best 16 blocks
  hold(block):
    granted                                      -> done
    conflict (row count < n, seat not free,
              lock not available, unique claim
              lost, document seat taken)         -> conflicts++; if conflicts == 20 give up;
                                                    re-read q01 of the same section
    retryable engine error (40001, 40P01)        -> jittered exponential backoff
                                                    (2 ms base, 50 ms cap, real time, not
                                                    scaled); retry the same block, bounded
                                                    by maxAttempts and the attempt deadline
```

The same seed and the same model are used for every design. A buyer's random stream never
depends on the design; the outcomes it meets do.

### 5.3 Harness-side conventions

- `hold_ms`, `payment_window_ms` and `app_now` are bound by the harness. `app_now` exists
  only in E0. There are two simulated application nodes: node 0 with skew 0, node 1 with
  `+lc-e0-skew` (in the race: the same skew in real time, which cannot fire against a
  40-minute TTL; the report states this). Buyers alternate nodes; the sweeper runs on node 1.
- Ticket ids for a confirmation are allocated before the transaction and bound as
  `ticket_ids BIGINT[]`, matched with `seat_ids`.
- Retries: `maxAttempts = 1000` and the attempt deadline, as study 02. Backoff applies only
  to retryable engine errors (§3.10), recorded as `engine_retries`.

### 5.4 Ledger

- **Seeded from the loaded state:** loaded live and expired holds with their intervals, and
  loaded tickets.
- **Per hold:** id, event, section, seats, customer, `granted_at`, `expires_at` (updated by

  margin / expired), ambiguous-commit flag.
- **Per seat:** ordered claims (hold intervals, sale, refund).
- **Confirmation timestamp:** the harness's own `SELECT now()` at transaction start, which
  gives rejected confirmations a timestamp too. It is the same instrumentation for every
  design, and its round trip is included in every design's confirmation latency. L2 uses the
  read's `db_now`.
- `ledger_test.go` covers at least these cases:
  1. a legitimate steal after expiry;
  2. a theft before expiry;
  3. release, then a re-hold;
  4. confirmed, then held (violation);
  5. refund, then a re-hold;
  6. a K1 extension, then a confirmation inside the window;
  7. margin classes at `m` = −1 ms, 0, `G` − 1 ms and `G`;
  8. a partial hold;
  9. an ambiguous-commit allowance.

### 6.3 S1 — `s1_conditional_update` (the reference)

`event_seat(event_id BIGINT REFERENCES event, seat_id INT, section_no INT, status TEXT CHECK
(status IN ('available','held','sold')), hold_id BIGINT, customer_id BIGINT,
hold_expires_at TIMESTAMPTZ, sold_at TIMESTAMPTZ, PRIMARY KEY (event_id, seat_id))`.

CHECKs:

- available ⇔ `hold_id`, `customer_id`, `hold_expires_at` and `sold_at` are all NULL;
- held ⇒ `hold_id`, `customer_id` and `hold_expires_at` NOT NULL, `sold_at` NULL;
- sold ⇒ `customer_id` and `sold_at` NOT NULL (`hold_id` is NULL for loaded tickets).

Indexes:

- `(event_id, section_no, seat_id)`;
- `(hold_expires_at ASC) WHERE status = 'held'` (sweeper).

The decisive statements:

```sql
-- name: w_hold_seats
-- params: hold_id, customer_id, hold_ms, event_id, section_no, seat_ids
UPDATE event_seat
   SET status = 'held', hold_id = $1, customer_id = $2,
       hold_expires_at = now() + make_interval(secs => $3::FLOAT8 / 1000.0)
 WHERE event_id = $4 AND section_no = $5 AND seat_id = ANY ($6::INT[])
   AND (status = 'available' OR (status = 'held' AND hold_expires_at <= now()))
RETURNING seat_id, now() AS db_now, hold_expires_at;

-- name: w_confirm_seats
-- params: event_id, section_no, hold_id, seat_ids
UPDATE event_seat
   SET status = 'sold', sold_at = now()
 WHERE event_id = $1 AND section_no = $2 AND seat_id = ANY ($4::INT[])
   AND hold_id = $3 AND status = 'held' AND hold_expires_at > now()
RETURNING seat_id, now() AS db_now;
```

Also required:

- `q01`: a held seat counts as held only while `hold_expires_at > now()`;
- `q02` and `q03`: count available as `status='available' OR (status='held' AND hold_expires_at <= now())`;
- `w_release_expired`: guarded by `status = 'held' AND hold_expires_at <= now()`;
- `w_publish_seats`: `INSERT INTO event_seat SELECT … FROM venue_seat WHERE venue_id = $`.

Comment in `schema.sql`: lazy expiry means correctness never depends on the sweeper, and
it also means no exact availability counter can be maintained, because an expiry changes
no row.

### 6.4 S2, S3 — lock first

S1's tables and statements, plus:

```sql
-- name: w_lock_seats
-- params: event_id, section_no, seat_ids
SELECT seat_id,
       (status = 'available' OR (status = 'held' AND hold_expires_at <= now())) AS free
  FROM event_seat
 WHERE event_id = $1 AND section_no = $2 AND seat_id = ANY ($3::INT[])
 ORDER BY seat_id
   FOR UPDATE;            -- S3: FOR UPDATE NOWAIT
```

Comment: `ORDER BY` before `FOR UPDATE` locks the rows in seat order, which prevents
deadlocks between overlapping blocks.

the expected failure: a second buyer's unconditional update waits for the first buyer's
commit, still matches, and silently overwrites the first hold. S4's must state why the same
SQL is correct at SERIALIZABLE: one of the two transactions fails with 40001 and retries.

Study 02's C1/C2 files were fully byte-identical. Here each file may carry one header
comment line naming its design; nothing else may differ.

### 6.6 E0 — application clock (control)

S1 with every `now()` in the logic replaced by `$app_now::TIMESTAMPTZ`, a parameter named
`app_now`. Every returning list keeps `now() AS db_now`. That includes `w_hold_seats`
(expiry computed as `$app_now + ttl`), `w_confirm_seats`, `w_check_hold`,
`w_release_expired`, `w_expired_holds` and `q01`–`q04`. The audit (`audit.sql`) keeps
`now()`: it judges the truth, so it uses the database clock. The correctness gate, the read
benchmark and the explain phase bind node 0's clock (no skew), so E0 is verified like every
other design.

Comment: on one host there is no real clock skew, so the skew is injected. The design
shows what judging expiry by an application server's clock does **when** servers disagree,
as they do across machines.

### 6.7 E1 — sweeper-materialised expiry

S1, with these differences:

- `w_hold_seats` condition: `AND status = 'available'`;
- `q01`: state from `status` alone;
- `q02` and `q03`: count `status = 'available'`;
- `w_check_hold` and `w_confirm_seats`: unchanged (the hold must still be unexpired).

The sweeper statements are S1's. Comment: every expired-but-unswept seat is unavailable
for the gap between expiry and the sweep, and indefinitely if the sweeper stops. The
lifecycle outage measures exactly that.

### 6.8 E2 — expiry on the cart

- `hold(hold_id BIGINT PK, customer_id BIGINT, event_id BIGINT, section_no INT, status TEXT
  CHECK (status IN ('held','confirmed','released')), expires_at TIMESTAMPTZ, created_at
  TIMESTAMPTZ)`.
- `event_seat` as in S1 but **without** `customer_id` and `hold_expires_at`; `hold_id`
  references `hold`.
- Index `event_seat(hold_id) WHERE status = 'held'`, and a sweeper index on
  `hold(expires_at ASC) WHERE status = 'held'`.

```sql
-- name: w_hold_seats
-- params: hold_id, event_id, section_no, seat_ids
UPDATE event_seat es
   SET status = 'held', hold_id = $1
 WHERE es.event_id = $2 AND es.section_no = $3 AND es.seat_id = ANY ($4::INT[])
   AND (es.status = 'available'
        OR (es.status = 'held'
            AND EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM hold h
                         WHERE h.hold_id = es.hold_id AND h.expires_at <= now())))
RETURNING es.seat_id, now() AS db_now;
```

- `w_confirm_cart`: `UPDATE hold SET status='confirmed' WHERE hold_id=$1 AND
  status='held' AND expires_at > now() RETURNING now() AS db_now`.
- `w_confirm_seats`: `… AND hold_id = $3 AND status = 'held'`, without an expiry
  predicate, because the cart holds it.

**Mandatory comment:** this design is correct **only because a cart's `expires_at` never
changes after it is written.** The seat update re-checks its own row after a lock wait, but
a subquery's view of another table can be stale (LESSONS_LEARNED, study 01 D9). Extending a
cart would require locking the cart row in every steal. That combination (E2 + K1) is not
measured.

### 6.9 K0 — naive confirmation (control)

S1, differing only in `w_confirm_seats`:

```sql
-- name: w_confirm_seats
-- params: event_id, section_no, hold_id, seat_ids
-- NAIVE: trusts the pre-payment check. If the hold expired during payment and
-- someone else holds or bought the seat, this overwrites them.
UPDATE event_seat
   SET status = 'sold', hold_id = $3, sold_at = now()
 WHERE event_id = $1 AND section_no = $2 AND seat_id = ANY ($4::INT[])
RETURNING seat_id, now() AS db_now;
```

`diff -r` against S1 must show `w_confirm_seats` and the header comment only.

### 6.10 K1 — payment window

S1, plus:

- a column `checkout_started_at TIMESTAMPTZ` on `event_seat`;
- `w_hold_seats` also sets `checkout_started_at = NULL`;
- a new statement:

```sql
-- name: w_begin_checkout
-- params: payment_window_ms, event_id, section_no, hold_id, seat_ids
UPDATE event_seat
   SET hold_expires_at = GREATEST(hold_expires_at,
                                  now() + make_interval(secs => $1::FLOAT8 / 1000.0)),
       checkout_started_at = now()
 WHERE event_id = $2 AND section_no = $3 AND seat_id = ANY ($5::INT[])
   AND hold_id = $4 AND status = 'held' AND hold_expires_at > now()
   AND checkout_started_at IS NULL
RETURNING seat_id, now() AS db_now, hold_expires_at;
```

The harness runs it in a transaction: rows = N → COMMIT, else ROLLBACK ("expired at
checkout"). The ledger takes the new `hold_expires_at`. `w_confirm_seats` is S1's.

### 6.11 L1 — claims created on hold

- `seat_claim(event_id BIGINT, seat_id INT, section_no INT, hold_id BIGINT, customer_id
  BIGINT, expires_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL, claimed_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL, PRIMARY KEY
  (event_id, seat_id))`.
- A **sold** claim has `expires_at = 'infinity'` (or the P4 fallback).
- `ticket` has **UNIQUE (event_id, seat_id)**; the comment explains that here it is the
  design's arbiter.
- There are no per-event seat rows. Publishing inserts the event row only.
- Indexes: `seat_claim(event_id, section_no, seat_id)`; sweeper
  `seat_claim(expires_at ASC)`.

```sql
-- name: w_claim_seats
-- params: event_id, section_no, hold_id, customer_id, hold_ms, seat_ids
INSERT INTO seat_claim AS c (event_id, seat_id, section_no, hold_id, customer_id, expires_at, claimed_at)
SELECT $1::BIGINT, s, $2::INT, $3::BIGINT, $4::BIGINT,
       now() + make_interval(secs => $5::FLOAT8 / 1000.0), now()
  FROM unnest($6::INT[]) AS s
ON CONFLICT (event_id, seat_id) DO UPDATE
   SET hold_id = EXCLUDED.hold_id, customer_id = EXCLUDED.customer_id,
       expires_at = EXCLUDED.expires_at, claimed_at = EXCLUDED.claimed_at
 WHERE c.expires_at <= now()
RETURNING seat_id, now() AS db_now, expires_at;
```

Other statements:

- `w_confirm_seats`: `UPDATE seat_claim SET expires_at='infinity' WHERE … AND
  hold_id=$3 AND expires_at > now() AND expires_at <> 'infinity' RETURNING …`;
- `w_release_hold`: `DELETE … WHERE hold_id = $ AND expires_at <> 'infinity'`;
- the sweeper deletes expired claims;
- `w_unsell_seat` deletes the sold claim;
- `q02` and `q03` use `venue_section.capacity` minus valid claims.

### 6.12 L2 — section document

- `event_section(event_id BIGINT, section_no INT, capacity INT, version BIGINT NOT NULL,
  claims JSONB NOT NULL, next_expiry TIMESTAMPTZ, PRIMARY KEY (event_id, section_no))`.
- `claims` is an object keyed by seat id (text):
  `{"<seat_id>": [state, hold_id, customer_id, expires_ms]}`. `state` is 1 for held and 2
  for sold; a sold claim has `expires_ms` = 0 and is judged by `state` alone. Available
  seats are absent.
- `next_expiry` is the minimum expiry among held claims (NULL if none), maintained by Go on
  every write.
- Sweeper index: `(next_expiry ASC)`.

Statements:

- `w_read_section(event_id, section_no)` → `version, claims, now() AS db_now`;
- `w_write_section(claims, next_expiry, event_id, section_no, version)` →
  `UPDATE … SET claims=$1, next_expiry=$2, version=version+1 WHERE event_id=$3 AND
  section_no=$4 AND version=$5 RETURNING now() AS db_now`;
- `w_check_hold` → Go from `w_read_section`;
- reads compute state in SQL with `jsonb_each` and `now()`;
- `w_publish_sections`: `INSERT … SELECT FROM venue_section` with an empty object.

Expiry semantics in Go:

- a claim is valid while `expires_ms > db_now_ms`, where `db_now_ms` comes from the read;
- a new hold's expiry is `read db_now + ttl`, and its `granted_at` is the write's `db_now`;
- the comment states that the hold is therefore a few milliseconds shorter than 40
  minutes, which errs in the buyer's disfavour and never towards theft.

Comment: this is study 01's embedding question (D6/D9) applied to seats. Every hold
rewrites the document of its section, serialising the whole section; the seat map becomes
a single-row read.

### 6.13 L3 — section-sharded (YugabyteDB only)

`(event_id, section_no, seat_id)` index is dropped as redundant. `diff -r` against S1 must
show `schema.sql`, `indexes.sql` and the header comment only. The runner never schedules L3

### AM-01 — Transient refusals on YugabyteDB, design S1r, dev-check criteria

**Reason.** On YugabyteDB, under the race's load, a guarded confirmation sometimes matches
none of a valid hold's seats. The identical statement, issued again in the same transaction,
matches all of them. This is a measured engine behaviour, not a harness bug, and it is exactly
the failure the owner asked about. The study measures it, keeps it apart from design failures,
and measures the smallest defence.

#### AM-01.1 Transient refusal (§3.4, §5.4)

- **Definition.** An early rejection (margin `m >= G`, §3.4) is **transient** when the
  identical refusing statement, issued again inside the refusing transaction before its
  rollback (AM-01.2), matches every seat the first execution did not. Every other early
  rejection is **persistent**.
- Both remain **INV-3 violations**. The buyer was refused, so the cells keep their ❌.
- Ledger: `Violations` gains `RejectedEarlyTransient` (JSON `rejected_early_transient`), a
  subset of `RejectedEarly`, summed by `Add`, **not** added again in `Violations()`. The
  `Reject` call takes the transient flag; the flag is ignored for late and boundary
  rejections.
- `ledger_test.go` adds:
  - 10: a transient early rejection counts in both totals;
  - 11: a transient flag on a late or boundary rejection changes nothing.

#### AM-01.2 Refusal diagnostics (§5.3)

Harness instrumentation, the same for every design it applies to. It replaces the
unconditional in-transaction re-read and re-issue added for the investigation (commit
`87fa9fa`).

- **Statements:** `w_confirm_seats` (S0–S4, E0, E1, K1, L1, L3), `w_confirm_cart` and
  `w_confirm_seats` (E2), `w_begin_checkout` (K1).
  - K0 has no guard: not applicable.
  - L2 checks in the application and writes by compare-and-set, and a lost write is retried
    (M9): not applicable, reported as "n/a".
- **When:** only if the ledger's margin for that hold at the transaction's `now()` is ≥ G.
  Check this before running anything, so late and boundary refusals take exactly the design's
  path.
- **What**, inside the refusing transaction, in this order:
  1. Re-read the hold's rows, with `pg_backend_pid()`.
     - Seat rows: the existing `seatStates`.
     - L1: `seat_id, hold_id, expires_at > now(), expires_at = 'infinity'` from `seat_claim`.
     - E2: the seat rows, plus the cart's `expires_at > now()`.
  2. The **identical statement with identical bindings**. If it matched `k` seats first and
     `k2` now, the refusal is transient when `k + k2 = N`. The guards exclude rows the first
     execution already changed (`status = 'held'`, `expires_at <> 'infinity'`,
