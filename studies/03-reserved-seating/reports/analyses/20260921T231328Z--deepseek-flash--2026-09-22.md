## Conclusions

**1. The layout result is real and correctly scoped.** On `yb-single` and `yb-cluster3`, L2/L3
answer the 100k-seat section map 4.6-11.4x faster than the per-seat row designs (verified:
`yb-single` 3 102 vs 679/273; `yb-cluster3` 4 294 vs 942/…), and on PostgreSQL all layouts sit
inside the run's ~1.8x error band (6.9k-10k ops/s), so the ordering there is not attributable.
The mechanism — one row per section versus hundreds of thousands of index lookups across the
YSQL-RPC boundary — is visible in the plans and is what a distributed engine should amplify.
The claim must carry "one `small` run, closed loop, 8 connections, quota-bound nodes".

**2. The document layouts move the cost, they do not remove it.** The same `yb-single` cell
that has L2 fastest at the seat map has it slowest at the point lookup (`r02` 1 520 vs ~4 900
for the row designs), and L3 collapses to **26.4 ops/s** — 57x worse than L2 and 185x worse than
the row designs; L3's `r01` is 408 ops/s against 3 451-4 040 for the row designs. The signed
analysis states this as a service-desk trade, not a benchmark win, and the run JSON supports it
exactly. This is the part of `claim-09` least likely to be quoted and most important to keep.

**3. r06 is the strongest result in the study and it is not a measurement.** The answerability
table marks the hold funnel `✗` in every design of every topology, and the reason is structural
(`w_release_expired` clears the row). The book can safely say "no design in this study can size
the hold window"; it cannot say the funnel is *slow*, because nothing measured it.

**4. The recency report repeats the cross-study shape.** `r05` runs at 251-253 ops/s on
PostgreSQL and 50-61 on YugabyteDB against 14k-20k for the buyer reads in the same cells — the
same 50-100x gap study 01 saw for donors and study 02 saw for customers. This is now a
three-domain observation and is the best-supported cross-domain mechanism in the corpus; it is
also the reason the studies' protocols should treat it as a maintained aggregate, not a query.

**5. Verification strength is adequate for a read-only run but not more.** The gate's 65/65 is
truth-checked against Go-computed answers on a load with sold seats, live holds and expired
holds, which is the right floor for a phase that writes nothing. It is not evidence that the
hold invariants are enforced under contention; that evidence lives in Study 03's v1 matrix and
is unaffected by this run.

**6. The claim verdicts are published separately.** `book/evidence/reviews/20260922-phase-3b-high-validation.json`
records `claim-09` and `claim-gap-03` **accepted**, `claim-08` **out of scope**, and
`claim-gap-04` **retired** by this validation pair. No correction of a Study 03 number is
required.

## Where I think the measurement is weak

- **No control fired** — `0 of 0` in the report, and the phases make firing impossible. The
  "none observed" invariant rows are load/answer audits only.
- **Single run, one trial per cell, 5 s per measurement, 8 connections, closed loop.** The
  report's own error bar (330 comparisons over ten designs reading identical SQL: median 1.02x,
  worst 1.79x) means differences below ~1.8x are not attributable; the L2/L3 claims are 5-185x
  and survive it, nothing at 1.1-1.5x does.
- **YugabyteDB cells are quota-bound**, so absolute ops/s describe a throttled 2-CPU node, not
  the engine.
- **The 100k tiers are slow enough that client effects are possible** (r05 at 50-61 ops/s with 8
  workers does very little per second); a single-worker repeat would separate client from
  database time.
- **No write/lifecycle phase ran**, so nothing here says what the reports cost while holds are
  being taken — the gap an operations desk would care about most.

## What I would measure next

3. **Reports during a drop** — r01/r02 concurrently with the sell-out race at 32 and 128 buyers,
   which is where a box office actually runs them.
