# Analysis — Study 05 v2 real-TTL churn — deepseek-flash

> Deliverable of `task-20260922T200831Z-study05-v2-churn-scale`, from

> PostgreSQL + Redis cells; no earlier analysis is edited.

## TL;DR

- **The v1 gap "the TTL was never crossed in a measured cell" is closed as a duration claim.** The
  600 s cells crossed **2.00** real 300 s boundaries and the 1800 s cell crossed **6.00**, with the
  churn result recording the boundaries actually spanned.
- **Correctness held through the churn.** Across 1.37M–2.88M blended reads per cell the wrong-read
  count is **0**, `impossible_values` is 0, the gate passed and no cell failed.
- **But the hard-expiry path fired zero times** (`expired_hard = 0`) in all three cells; only
  probabilistic early expiry fired (11,321–37,063). The cache never let an entry reach the hard
  300 s deadline, so *crossing the boundary in time* is measured while *observing the hard TTL
  expire an entry* is not. That distinction is the honest result of this task.
- Refreshes are not recorded by the harness, the 5×-for-120 s burst has no write-rate knob, and the
  requested 8k/80k/800k scales exceed the harness's largest scale (3,000 people / ~120k donations).

## 2. What the numbers mean

The churn phase runs a sustained blended read/write workload for the requested wall-clock duration
against a cache with a fixed 300 s TTL; `hard_ttl_boundaries_crossed` is elapsed/300. So the cells
genuinely spent 2 and 6 TTL lifetimes under load, and under that load the strict owned model served
every read from a state the ledger agreed with (`wrong_reads = 0`). That is the correctness claim v1
could not make over a real boundary.

The expiry counters say something narrower than the duration: `expired_hard` is the count of entries
observed to be **gone because their hard TTL elapsed**, and it is zero. Every expiry recorded is
probabilistic early expiry (the design evicts/refreshes before the deadline). So this task shows the
system stays correct when the clock passes the TTL many times; it does **not** show an entry sitting
until 300 s and the hard expiry removing it. A cell that disables early expiration, or a workload
that stops refreshing a subset, would be needed for that.

## 4. Where this measurement is weak

1. **One topology and one scale.** PostgreSQL + Redis, scale small; no YugabyteDB and no medium
   scale, so nothing here says how the boundary behaves with replication or a larger working set.
2. **Hard expiry unexercised.** See section 2; the zero is a finding, not a confirmation.
3. **Closed loop.** The blended workload is closed-loop, so read volumes are a floor, and the wrong-read
   rate is measured against the ledger, not against an external observer.
4. **One run each.** No repeat of a churn cell; the counters are single-run.
