## TL;DR

- **The strongest results survive an independent re-derivation.** `x1`'s loss is exact by
  construction (3 416 acknowledged, counter 215, **3 201 lost = 93.7%**); the rollup read gain is
  15.2x/15.8x (`r04` 867 → 13 197/13 693 ops/s) against a **6.85x** `w05` write cost (573 → 83.7
  ops/s, p99 2.5 → 42 ms); the document storage win is a deterministic `pg_total_relation_size`
  comparison, 1 032 192 B → 98 304 B (**10.5x**), untouched by any timing artefact.
- **The 1.76x lock-vs-version result is accepted only as an upper bound.** The author's own
  mechanism discussion says the harness may charge `c1` extra per-retry work and that
  `-retries 1` was not run; the claim must carry that, plus one key / one installation / 16
  writers / single trial / PostgreSQL only. It is not a read-phase number, so the ~1.7x read
  floor does not apply to it — the one-trial limit does.
- **The identical-SQL spread is real; the proposed cause is not corroborated by this run's own
  order.** The three byte-identical reads span 1.59-1.66x (`n1` slowest, `x1` fastest in two of
  three). But `n1` is the *eighth* cell and the *tenth* (`n0`) is mid-range, while `n3`/`n4` (6th,
  7th) are slower than `n2`/`n0` (9th, 10th): "later cell = dirtier database = slower" would not
  produce that. The defensive reading — no read difference below ~1.7x is attributable — is right
  and should be kept; the *explanation* needs the repeated-design control, not the order.
- **The "65%" label is presentation, not measurement.** The discussion's precise table gives
  1.66x (r01), 1.64x (r02), 1.59x (r03); the analysis's parenthetical example is r03 (1.59x) while
  the headline figure comes from r01. Small wording fix; the numbers agree.
- **The gaps are coverage gaps, not null results, and they are already mapped.**
  `claim-gap-01` is accepted, and `task-20260922T025912Z-study04-v2-protocol` already names
  missing designs, repeated randomized trials, cardinality, cadence/churn, equal-total resources,
  placement and YugabyteDB. This review adds three specific requirements to that protocol rather
  than creating a new task.

## Conclusions

**1. Accept the control and storage findings; they are the most solid things in the run.** `x1`'s
93.7% loss is measured against an expectation derived from acknowledged operations, not from the
value the client wrote — the right reconciliation shape, and the reason the second control (`x2`,
60 rollup mismatches) also fires. The storage comparison is not a timing and is exact. These three
findings (control fires, rollup read/write trade, document size) are the ones a reader can act on.

**2. Narrow the concurrency claim to an upper bound and a shape.** The recorded ratio is 1.76x
(887 vs 503 ops/s), and the direction is physically expected: a version check converts contention
into discarded transactions, and 545 retries for 2 065 successes is the visible cost. But the
run cannot separate "the lock is cheaper" from "the harness charges the retry loop more", because
`-retries 1` was not run; the author says so. As stated, `claim-10`'s first clause should read
that the lock delivered 1.76x *on this one key at 16 writers in one trial, an upper bound on its
advantage*. Nothing in the run supports a low-contention comparison, and the analysis correctly
refuses one.

**3. The rollup trade is the study's clearest design result.** A trigger-maintained overview buys
`r04` 15.2x and pays 6.85x on whole-configuration replacement (83.7 vs 573 ops/s) with p99 2.5 →
42 ms, while the application rollup keeps `w05` at 593 ops/s (within ~4% of the reference) for the
same 15.8x read. The review accepts the framing that the trigger's real product is a structural
guarantee (the write path cannot forget it) and that `x2` demonstrates the failure when the
application maintenance is not in the publishing transaction. Both halves are measured here.

**4. Keep the attribution floor; replace the proposed mechanism with a control.** The three
identical-SQL reads are `r01` 9 148 → 15 141 (1.655x), `r02` 16 308 → 26 801 (1.643x), `r03`
25 114 → 40 010 (1.593x), all with `n1` slowest. That is a real instrument problem and the ~1.7x
floor is the right consequence. What this run cannot support is the *cause*: the buffer/catalog
story predicts a monotone penalty with cell position, and the recorded order does not show one —
the slowest identical-SQL cell is 8th of 10, and the last two cells (`n2`, `n0`) beat cells 6-8 at
those reads. The distinguishing experiment is a repeated design (`n1` first and last, three
trials), exactly as the author proposes; until it runs, the mechanism should be labelled a
hypothesis, as the analysis already does, and the floor must be applied to *every* read difference
below 1.7x — including the `n0`-faster-than-`n1` observation, which is not a finding.

**5. The missing designs and topologies are gaps, not null results.** `d2`, `d3`, `d4`, `h1`,
`s1`, `s2`, `y1`, `y2` are unbuilt; there is no YugabyteDB cell, no network, no colocation, no
equal-total-resource control, and no repeated conclusion run. The review expressly does not read
"the missing designs would not change this" into any conclusion, and `claim-gap-01` is accepted.

**6. The v2 protocol is the right vehicle; it needs three additions.** No new task is created —
`task-20260922T025912Z-study04-v2-protocol` (currently `proposed`, depending on this review)
already carries the acceptance criteria for the gaps. This review requires the protocol to add,
explicitly: (a) a **repeated-design instrument control** (`n1` first and last, ≥3 trials, fresh
database per cell or a recorded randomized order) so the read floor is measured rather than
assumed; (b) the **`-retries 1` control** on the `c1`/`c2` contention pair so the lock's
advantage is separated from harness charging; (c) an explicit statement that the 1.76x contention
number is a single point on a writer sweep, not a curve. Items (a)-(c) are cheap, artifact-only
protocol additions and do not authorize measurement here.

**7. One presentation correction, not a measurement one.** The signed analysis's "65% spread
among identical SQL" is the 1.66x `r01` comparison, while its parenthetical example is the 1.59x
`r03` one. A reader checking the example against the percentage will be confused; the discussion's
table is the precise statement. The v2 protocol should restate the floor in one place for both.

## Where I think the measurement is weak

- **One trial per measurement, every in-report spread 0.0.** The methodology's rule 8 applies in
  full: nothing here is a design's throughput, only a direction. The two ratios the review accepts
  (rollup read, rollup write) are large enough to survive one trial; the 1.76x is not.
- **The instrument itself drifts.** The 1.6x identical-SQL spread means the read table's small
  differences — the document read win, the index-control reversal — are unproven, and the author
  says so. The review's finding is that the *explanation* is also unproven.
- **Single node, shared laptop cores, no network or replication.** All environment hazards apply;
  absolute ops/s are not capacity.
- **`x1`'s 93.7% is a workload property** (16 writers, one key), not a law; at low contention the
  unchecked path loses nothing.
- **The rollup write penalty is measured on `w05_replace_all`** (60-key replacement); its
  curvature across smaller publishes is in the same table but not analysed.

## What I would measure next

1. **Instrument control:** `n1` twice in one matrix (first and last), three trials, fresh schema
   per cell; report the first/last gap against the `n1`-`x1` gap.
2. **Retry control:** the `c1`/`c2` contention cell with `-retries 1` (and a writer sweep 1/2/4/8/
   16/32), to turn the 1.76x upper bound into a curve.
3. **Cardinality:** `n0` vs `n1` at 1/10/30/60/120/500 entries, totals held constant, which is
   what the index control was built for and this run could not test.
4. **Cadence/churn** on a fleet-scale dataset, which is the only way to price the maintenance
   mechanisms under arrival that is not a single sequential pass.

These are the phase E/F/G items already in `HANDOFF.md`; they belong to the v2 protocol task.
