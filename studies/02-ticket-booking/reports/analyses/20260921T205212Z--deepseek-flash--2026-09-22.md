## Conclusions

**1. AM-03.2/AM-03.5/AM-04 acceptance is met, and the evidence is inspectable.** The ledger
audit runs after every writing phase in the measured runs, not just in dev checks: 3b-3 has 26
ledger audits in each of the six X1/P3 cells, all consistent (`mismatches=0`,
`attribution_mismatches=0`); 3b-2 has 26/26/18, all consistent (the 18 is the partial cell).
Post-write report checks pass everywhere (3 for P3, 6 for X1 per cell). The audit's *power* is
demonstrated separately and correctly: the injected `-ledger-fault drop-sale` and
`wrong-customer` controls are seen to fail the gate in `am04-dc01-pg/fault-*.log`, and the
pre-AM-04 natural control (`step1-natural-control-unfixed.log`) shows the audit fires on the
unfixed SQL. This is exactly the shape AM-03.3 asked for: controls prove the instrument, and the
measured runs then use it.

**2. The 128-buyer pair is the claim to quote; the 32-buyer pair is evidence about the
machine.** 3b-3's ratios come from tight trials on both designs (1-37% spreads) and its
PostgreSQL ordering (P3 faster at every tier) is the physically expected one, since X1 does
strictly more work per sale. 3b-2's PostgreSQL ordering is the opposite and its P3 trials
disagree by 92-283%, which the analysis correctly refuses to interpret. Because both arms are
internally consistent and disagree with each other, the honest published statement is the
128-buyer one, explicitly scoped: "at 128 buyers, one `small` run, closed loop, PostgreSQL
1-node / YugabyteDB 1-node and 3-node". Neither arm supports a general "the ledger halves the
drop" statement, because on 3b-2's own tables the YugabyteDB cost ranges from 1.7x to 5.7x.

**3. The refund is the expensive half, and the mechanism is engine-specific.** Isolated cancel
in 3b-3 falls 2.1x on `yb-single` (575→271/s) and 2.5x on `yb-cluster3` (404→164/s) while book
falls only 1.5x/1.4x, and it is the only isolated write that changed sign on PostgreSQL
(4595→5232/s, i.e. inside the noise). That is consistent with AM-04.2's statement of the pair:
the refund path adds the locking read the ledger insert needs to name the buyer, which costs a
round trip on the distributed engine and is lost in the noise locally. A reader should quote
the refund cost per engine, never as one number.

**4. Answerability, not throughput, is Study 02's durable result — and it is a schema fact.**
The reports matrix (`20260920T234953Z`) shows `r05` (refunds in a window) unanswerable and
`r01`/`r03` partial in 14 of 15 designs, because cancellation erases the sale; X1's append-only
`sale_event` is the only design that answers all three correctly. This is a data-model finding
that indexes cannot fix, and it is the one Study 02 number that transfers to other domains.

**5. Two derived claims need correction before the book uses them.** These are recorded as
review verdicts in `book/evidence/reviews/20260922-phase-3b-high-validation.json` and are
corrections of *derivation*, not re-interpretation of the measurements:

- `claim-07` (from 3b-2's headline) prints "1.7-2.1x". The run's own tables give 1.7-5.7x, and
  1.7-2.1x is the 3b-3 range. Widen the range to the run's data, or scope the clause to the
  1k/10k/100k tiers; either way it must not stand as written.
- `claim-05`'s "8x load time" is not reproducible: the same pair and code measure 1.2x in the
  two sibling runs. Keep "+33-35% storage" and the answerability result; drop the load
  multiplier or re-measure it. (The reports run also cannot attribute P3→X1 *read* rows, as its
  own analysis conclusion 6 says — the controlled-pair read rows there must not be quoted.)

**6. The v2 validation question in current context is answered.** The phase-3b claims are now
*complete and HIGH-validated* with the qualifications above. `claim-gap-04` ("validation is
still open") is no longer true and is retired by this review. No measurement is authorized or
needed to close it.

## Where I think the measurement is weak

- **No negative control ran in any of the three measured runs** (all report "0 of 0 fired").
  The controls' sensitivity is demonstrated only in `tiny` dev checks, not in the `small`
  regime whose correctness is claimed. The clean audits are strong evidence *about X1* and weak
  evidence about the audit's sensitivity at `small` contention.
- **One run per arm, one trial per tier in the design-level sense.** The 3-trial spreads are
  within a cell; nothing repeats a whole cell, so a per-cell systematic effect — which is
  exactly what the PostgreSQL sign flip looks like — is invisible.
- **Quota-bound engines.** The database container is throttled for most of every cell and X1's
  cells are longer, so the throughput ratios are "how each design uses a throttled core", not
  engine limits. Absolute sold/s must never be quoted as capacity.
- **The partial cell.** `yb-cluster3/x1_cas_ledger` in 3b-2 has 2-trial medians and is missing
  its third race tier; its ratios are correspondingly softer.
- **Closed loop, shared laptop cores, no network between the "three nodes".** Tails are a floor,
  and the 3-node result is a placement result, not a distributed-systems one.
- **The 100k tier times out on both YugabyteDB topologies**, so the largest drop is measured
  partially (30-31% sold for P3, 15-16% for X1).
- **The load-time instability itself is unexplained.** I can show the 6x swing is not a code
  change (empty harness diff), but not what caused it; a controlled load-only repeat would.

## What I would measure next

1. **Interleave P3 and X1 tier-by-tier inside one cell**, at 32 *and* 128 buyers, to convert
   the PostgreSQL ratio from "between 1x and 2.3x" into an attributable number. This is the one
   experiment that retires the order confound the two analyses already name.
2. **Put `c1_count_naive` (and `h0_hold_naive_confirm`) into the race run** so the audit is seen
   to catch overbooking in the same `small` regime whose correctness is claimed.
3. **A load-only repeat of the P3/X1 pair** (verify phase only, `small`, pg-single, several
   repetitions) to decide whether the ledger's load cost is 1.2x or 8x. Nothing else in the
   phase is as cheap to settle.
4. **Refund-heavy churn** (50%, 100%) on `yb-single`, measuring the sweep-up funnel, not just
   throughput, since the refund is where the ledger's cost concentrates.
