# Study 01 v3 — D2/D3 follow-up conclusions

## TL;DR

- **Use the copied charity key together with the SQL and indexes that exploit it** when
  charity feeds and totals matter. D3 beat D2 in all five paired read trials; its median
  twelve-query score was 1.75× D2's. Copying the key alone did not reproduce the gain.
- **A covering index helped substantially after explicit vacuum preparation.** Adding
  `INCLUDE (amount_cents)` at identical SQL raised charity-sum throughput 3.29×; saved
  plans actually recorded zero heap fetches.
- **Choose against the real workload.** Donor-history reads were similar and D3's global
  sum was slower. D3 used more storage, and short insert trials remain too variable for
  a precise write-cost claim.

## Scope and evidence

The [generated report](../20260913T125342Z-v3.md) contains **100 cells**, with no initial
gate failures, operation errors or recorded post-write invariant failures. Each of the
20 conditions/design groups has five independently loaded trials. This analysis covers
the repeated PostgreSQL comparison and mechanism controls, not the separate growth or
YugabyteDB follow-ups.

The dataset has 108,081 donations, 5,000 donors and 10 charities. PostgreSQL 17.11 ran
with 2 CPUs/3 GiB; the client had 2 CPUs/2 GiB on the same laptop. Read trials received
explicit vacuum preparation; writes and blended trials began on separate fresh loads.

## Conclusions

**The repeated advantage is concentrated in specific questions.** D3's median score
was **8,897.79 versus D2's 5,071.75**, with within-design spreads of 9.1% and 15.2%.
The score is the geometric mean of twelve isolated query rates, then the median across
trials; it is not application requests/s. Paired D3/D2 ratios ranged from **1.53 to
1.83**. In the separately measured blend of four readers and 500 scheduled inserts/s,
D3 delivered **3,916 versus 2,320 reads/s**, with no rejected writes and all acknowledged
inserts reconciled.

**Rewriting the charity feed unlocks the recency index.** At identical indexes, D12→D13
raised its median throughput from **1,081 to 13,716/s**. The smallest-charity D2/D3
comparison was particularly large: **535 versus 16,066/s**. That result depends on key
selectivity; it should not be assigned to every charity or donor query. The complete
[SQL, plan and design comparison](../discussions/d2-d3-mechanisms--gpt-6--2026-09-13.md)
explains the reduced index walk and the ranking rewrites separately.

**Treat heap avoidance as an observed condition.** D14→D15 changed only the sum index's
included amount, producing **759→2,496 charity sums/s** after vacuum. Both bracketing
plans showed zero heap fetches for both targeted charity sizes in all five D15 trials.
ANALYZE-only diagnostics changed during the window and had wide variation. The companion
explains why this strengthens the prepared-read finding without contradicting the
earlier analysis's observation of 31,293 heap fetches.

**Do not apply the package indiscriminately.** Donor-history rates were similar
(35,417 versus 36,500/s), and global sums were slower in D3 (340 versus 248/s).
Median initial relation size increased from **18.13 to 25.70 MiB**. Insert medians were
7,752 versus 6,319/s, but D3's **49.3% spread** makes a precise percentage cost premature.

## Where I think the measurement is weak

Read and isolated insert windows were only three seconds, on shared laptop cores and
memory-resident data. Design order alternated within blocks rather than being fully
randomized; query order stayed fixed. Spread is a descriptive range, not statistical
confidence. The 15-second arrival trials locate neither saturation nor a production SLO;
their percentiles cover successful requests and use a bounded queue.

D11 copies the key **and adds its FK**, so its overhead is not a pure column cost.
The initial gate does not prove copied-key consistency during donor reassignment.
I implemented and ran these enhancements and read the earlier analyses: this is an
extension by the implementer, not a blinded independent replication.

## Next measurements and analyst exchange

Use longer paired insert windows, test the covering index under sustained corrections,
and weight reads/charity keys using an actual workload. Growth, contention and distributed
deployment results belong to their own run and analysis.
