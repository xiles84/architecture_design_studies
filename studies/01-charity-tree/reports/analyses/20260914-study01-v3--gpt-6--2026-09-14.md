# Study 01 v3 — enhancement conclusions

## TL;DR

- **D3's charity-read advantage is repeatable.** The copied key needs suitable indexes
  and rewritten SQL; the five-load median read score was 1.75× D2's.
- **Memory configuration changes the donor-read winner.** On identical data, D6 led
  at 256 MiB; D3 led at 3 GiB. Cross-donor aggregation still favored D3.
- **Choose rollups for the questions and contention pattern.** Lock-first D5 outperformed
  the full trigger on overloaded inserts; sum-only D16 still contends on the parent row.
- **D9 remains invalid; D10 passed the exercised YugabyteDB cache audits.** The tested
  contract checks ordered IDs, with broader payload and mixed-mutation coverage still needed.
- **One larger YugabyteDB node won this local equal-budget comparison.** Three nodes
  preserved acknowledged counts after a local node stop, with substantial service delay.

## What changed and what was measured

Seven new designs separate D3's mechanisms and add sum-only maintenance. The harness
now retains independent fresh loads, preparation and bracketing plans, deterministic
growth phases, and scheduled arrivals with queueing, rejection and reconciliation.
The first 14 cross-engine verification cells passed. The five-trial mechanism matrix
had 100 successful cells. The 135-cell stress/deployment matrix had six expected D9
cache-control failures; its other 129 cells passed recorded gates and audits. All twelve
matched-memory cells passed their initial and 108 post-mutation gates. Across the four
runs, 261 cells completed, including those six invalid negative controls.

## Conclusions

**Keep the D3 changes together when charity feeds and sums justify them.** The earlier
[concise D2/D3 analysis](20260913T125342Z-v3--gpt-6--2026-09-13.md) establishes the repeated
advantage and its storage cost. At identical SQL, adding the covering amount raised
charity sums **759→2,496/s** after explicit vacuum preparation. The
[mechanism discussion](../discussions/d2-d3-mechanisms--gpt-6--2026-09-13.md) separates the
key, indexes, rewrites and actual heap avoidance. Unchanged donor reads did not inherit
the whole score improvement, and short insert trials remained variable.

**Embedding remains expensive for long mutable histories and cross-donor aggregation.**
At standard memory with the longer-history dataset, final-phase donor reads were
**27,498/s for D3 versus 6,738/s for D6**; global sums were **27.43 versus 5.06/s**.
On a matched two-million-donation dataset, however, D6's final donor-read median led
**16,034 versus 4,578/s at 256 MiB**, while D3 led **25,223 versus 15,415/s at 3 GiB**.
That exception depends on the memory configuration and query; it does not remove
embedding's aggregation and mutation costs. See
[embedding and growth](../discussions/embedding-growth--gpt-6--2026-09-14.md).

**Lock-first application code can beat a full trigger for an insert-only workload.**
With 90% of writes directed to ten donors in one charity and 2,500 offered inserts/s,
D5 completed a median **433/s**, D4 **247/s**, and D16 **241/s**. All rejected most
arrivals at that rate. D5 still lacks correction/delete maintenance, and the simpler
sum trigger does not remove shared-parent contention. Highly variable isolated
mutations prevent a precise extrema-cost claim.
[Full comparison and rejection counts](../discussions/parent-contention--gpt-6--2026-09-14.md).

**Treat the foreign-key and cache findings as operation-specific.** Without FKs,
single-node YB inserts were **449 versus 357/s**, a 1.26× ratio of medians. Longer
three-node erasure gave **136 versus 122/s**; the earlier roughly 1.51× gap did not
repeat. Removing integrity checks therefore has a measured but narrower benefit.
D9 failed all six concurrent-insert cache trials; D10 passed them, with additional
correction/delete maintenance cost on YB.
[Evidence and audit boundaries](../discussions/yugabyte-controls--gpt-6--2026-09-14.md).

**Equal resources do not make this cluster a throughput upgrade.** One 6 CPU/9 GiB YB
node had **2.37× the read score and 4.21× the isolated insert rate** of three nodes
sharing the same total budget. Cluster SQL used only n1, whose CPU quota was heavily
throttled while peers had spare capacity. This is a deployment result for that access
pattern, not a pure replication-cost estimate. Stopping n3 preserved acknowledged
counts in all three recovery checks, but rejected about 42% of scheduled requests and
produced successful-response p99 near **15 seconds**.
[Deployment controls and failure details](../discussions/yugabyte-controls--gpt-6--2026-09-14.md).

## Where I think the measurement is weak

All containers share one laptop; CPU quotas, short read windows, fixed query order and
three-trial stress comparisons limit precision. Successful-request tails exclude
rejections, which must be read alongside them. Cache payloads, donor reassignment,
real networks and physical-host failure are not established. I implemented and ran the
enhancements, so this is not a blinded independent replication.

## What to measure next

Use balanced YB query endpoints, full cache-payload audits under overlapping mutations,
and longer mutation/covering-index trials. Independent hosts are required for the
remaining real-network and endpoint/host-failure tests described in
[the protocol](../../ENHANCEMENTS.md). Include a matching no-fault arrival condition.
